## Users

**Founders of AI and robotics startups first** (operator, 2026-11-14). Their situation: a small
team putting an AI system or a robot on the EU market, facing the AI Act, the Machinery
Regulation, the Cyber Resilience Act, the GDPR, the Data Act and product liability at once. Their
job: find out what applies to what they build, from when, and where exactly it says so — and
take that to counsel.

Served by the same pages:

- **Investors** — what exposure a company carries and what lands in the next years.
- **Companies coming to the EU** from the US and elsewhere — which rules reach them from outside,
  what they must set up to enter.
- **Curious minds** — anyone learning how the EU regulates technology.
- **Their agents** — consulting the map over MCP, passing on its citations.

## Product Purpose

An open, structured map of EU technology regulation, with AI, data and robotics at the core.
EU regulation is complex — a kingdom of bureaucracy — and founders give up in front of it; the
map inverts that: easy access in plain words, and agentic support, so people building in Europe
do not give up and find what they need (vision, `docs/concept/01-vision.md`).

Success: a founder answers "what applies to us, from when" in minutes, every line linked to an
article; agents use the MCP server and cite it; others reuse the records as an open dataset.

## Positioning

Not a copy of EUR-Lex. EUR-Lex and Cellar hold the texts and their metadata; the map adds what
neither has: **obligations broken down** by who, what, from when and where; **the dates that
apply per part** after amendments; **questions across acts** through a small shared vocabulary;
views by role, product and date; plain-words explanations; guidance outside the Official
Journal; and an MCP interface. **Every fact carries its citation, or it is not served**
(`docs/concept/00-introduction.md`).

## Operating Context

- People **browse** the site: one page per act, views by role, product and date, a timeline, a
  relation map, search.
- People **bring their own agent** over MCP; the map serves cited facts and the agent reasons
  over them. There is no model on our side.
- Answers are taken to counsel, data rooms and memos: a citation must survive being copied.
- Every MCP fact carries its EUR-Lex source, the act that set it, a permalink back to the map,
  its record id, read date, state and the bundle version, plus a copyable CC BY 4.0 attribution
  line (`docs/concept/04-architecture.md`, section 5).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Information, not legal advice.** The map reports what a text says, to whom, from when and
  where; it never judges the reader's situation ("you must", "you are compliant"). Said on every
  page and in every answer.
- **Independent, not affiliated with the European Union** — said on every page next to the
  not-advice notice (the domain is `.eu`).
- **Cite or stay silent**; **point, don't copy** — link to EUR-Lex, quote only short passages.
- **Unchecked facts are never served**; stale facts are served with a marker.
- English only, with links to every language version on EUR-Lex.
- First acts: AI Act (phase 1), GDPR, Data Act, Machinery Regulation, Cyber Resilience Act,
  Product Liability Directive. National law out at first.
- Terms: act, article, obligation, record, consolidated version, CELEX, ELI — defined in the
  glossary (`docs/concept/00-introduction.md`).

## Product Principles

1. **Cite or stay silent** — the citation is the product; a claim without one is not shown.
2. **Information, not advice** — say what the text says and where; leave the judgment to the
   reader and their counsel.
3. **Courage through clarity** — the reader should leave less afraid of EU regulation than they
   arrived.
4. **The same answer for people and agents** — one bundle, one index, one set of citations.
5. **Show the gaps** — unchecked, stale and not-covered are visible, never hidden.
